Last crit of the semester, and the final one from the vote. In
[How you stopped](/sessions/06-how-you-stopped/) the room produced a category
nobody had named in advance. Ending the uncertainty. Just asking, just
sending, just finding out. It got more votes than anything else in the thread,
so it gets the last session.

## Before the session

Bring a loop you are currently in that contains a prediction. Something you
believe will happen, or has happened, that you have not checked.

They usually hide inside a feeling. "They are annoyed with me" is a
prediction. "I will freeze if I ask a question in that seminar" is a
prediction. "This email will read as rude" is a prediction. "I am bad at
this" is not, at least not yet, and part of the work is getting it there.

## In the session

**First, make it falsifiable.** In pairs, turn the loop into a sentence that
could turn out to be wrong. The test is simple. If you cannot describe what
evidence would change your mind, you do not have a prediction, you have a
mood, and the rest of the hour will not work on it.

**Second, design the cheapest test.** Not the definitive one. The cheapest.
What is the smallest, fastest, least alarming thing that would give you real
information? Usually it is a question, a message, a two-minute attempt, or
asking one person. Write down what you expect to happen, with a number if you
can. Sixty percent sure they are annoyed. Write down what would count as the
prediction failing, before you run it.

**Third, some of you run it.** There is a chunk of the session set aside for
exactly this. Send the message. Ask the question. Try the thing you have been
rehearsing. Then come back and tell the room what happened.

This round is genuinely optional and a lot of people will sit it out. Passing
is normal here, you do not have to say why, and choosing not to run a test on
a real relationship in a classroom is frequently the correct call rather than
avoidance. If you prefer, design the test and run it in your own time, or not
at all.

## What we are actually testing

The course has spent eleven weeks on the inside of the loop. Conditions,
breaks, attention, the empty slot. This session is about the other exit, the
one that skips all of that and goes and gets the information instead.

It is worth being straight about the comparison. Nobody here has run a study
putting "learn to sit with uncertainty" against "go and resolve the
uncertainty" in a cohort of postgraduate students, and neither has anyone
else as far as we can find. What we have is eleven weeks of your own data and
one very consistent finding from week 6, which is that the exits that worked
on you were mostly endings.

Whether that makes finding out the best technique in the course or just the
one that skips the course is the argument to have with the time left.
