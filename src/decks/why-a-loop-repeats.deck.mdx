# Why a loop repeats

The claim the course is named after.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

# A loop repeats because it never gets the conditions under which thinking finishes

---

## Not all breaks are the same break

Baird and colleagues (2012, *Psychological Science*): a creativity task, an
incubation period, then the task again.

| During the break | Improvement |
| --- | --- |
| An undemanding task | **Yes — substantially** |
| A demanding task | No |
| Rest, nothing to do | No |
| No break at all | No |

So "take a break" is bad advice. Doing nothing did not help.

---

## What helped was oddly specific

An easy task that occupies your hands and leaves your attention loose.

Washing dishes. Walking a familiar route. Standing in a shower.

**The asterisk:** only *constructive* mind-wandering looks like this.
Ruminative mind-wandering tracks with anxiety, not incubation. The same idle
mind does both.

---

## Unfinished things come back

Leroy (2009) named **attention residue**: switch away before finishing and
part of your attention stays stuck on what you left. Worse when it was
unresolved.

Which gives the repetition its mechanism. A spiral returns not because it is
important but because it is *unfinished*.

## Deny a thought the conditions to finish and it does not resolve — it re-queues

---

## The blank that isn't

You spiral, you scroll, your head feels *blank*, and it feels like relief.

Kang and Kurtzberg (2019), 414 people, break activity and length held
constant — only the device changed:

- phone break: ~19% longer, ~22% fewer problems solved
- **roughly the same as no break at all**

A break can feel like a break and measure like nothing.

---

## What the course will not claim

- **Effects are small.** Orben and Przybylski (2019): technology use explains
  at most ~0.4% of variance in adolescent wellbeing.
- **Causation is unresolved.** Anxious people plausibly scroll more.
- **The blank itself is unstudied.** People report it; nobody has measured it.

"Doomscrolling made overthinking worse" is a claim this course does not make.

---

## And this is a hunch

Baird is a finding. Leroy is a finding.

"A spiral repeats because the incubation slot is occupied" is an inference
across the two that **nobody has tested directly** — with an obvious rival
explanation: people who feel stuck may seek distraction rather than the
reverse.

Argue with it. That is what it is for.
