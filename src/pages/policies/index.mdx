# Policies and support

Every rule here exists because a class actually ran into it. They are short on
purpose.

## AI

**This course is strictly against using AI to produce your assessed work.**

The reason is not integrity theatre. This course is about *your* thinking —
noticing your own loop, in your own words, and finding out what stops it. An
account of what you overthought, written by a language model, is not a lighter
version of that work. It is the absence of it, and the only person it takes
anything from is you.

So, concretely:

| Using AI for | Allowed? |
| --- | --- |
| Grammar, spelling, tidying up your formatting | **Yes** |
| Making a submission read clearly and present neatly | **Yes** |
| Researching techniques that help with overthinking, to bring to class | **Yes — encouraged** |
| Writing your log entries, your forum post, or your capstone | **No** |
| Asking it what you are overthinking, or what to think about it | **No** |

The middle row is the interesting one. Going and finding out what the internet
recommends for rumination is genuinely useful, and if a model points you at a
technique nobody here has heard of, bring it — say where it came from, we will
look at whether the evidence stacks up, and if it does it goes on the list the
room votes on. Research is thinking. Outsourcing the noticing is not.

The rest of this site tries to model what we want from you: every claim
checked, every source named, and the guesses labelled as guesses. Do that and
you will be fine.

## Respect

This course asks people to say out loud that they re-read a message forty
times. That only works in a room where nobody will be made to regret it. So
this is the one rule here with no dry humour attached to it.

**Be respectful and considerate. Bullying, mockery, or any deliberately
harmful behaviour will be acted on seriously, and quickly.** That includes the
Ed forum, the anonymous threads, group work, and anything that happens on the
way out of class. Mocking what someone admitted, quoting a post outside the
forum, pressuring anyone to explain a pass, or using anonymity as cover all
count.

Anonymity is not a loophole. Posts in
[the anonymous thread](/assessments/the-anonymous-thread/) are anonymous to
other students and to markers, but a moderator can identify an author when a
post is reported. That is a deliberate design decision: a forum where nobody
could ever be identified would be a forum with no way to protect the people
being honest in it.

**If something happens to you, or you see it happen, report it** — to a tutor,
the convenor, or by reporting the post on the forum. You do not have to be
certain, you do not have to have it fully worked out, and you will not be
asked to handle it yourself. Reporting something and being wrong costs you
nothing.

## Attendance

Attendance is part of your mark — see [Showing up](/assessments/showing-up/)
for how it works and for an honest account of the evidence for requiring it.
The practical version:

- Your best **9 of 12** weeks count in full, so three misses cost nothing and
  need no explanation.
- **If you are ill or something has come up, tell the lecturer beforehand.** An
  email or a message before class is all it takes, and it is easier for
  everyone than reconstructing it later.
- Past three misses with a real reason — illness, caring, a life event, a body
  that does not run to a timetable — talk to the convenor. A mark for turning
  up should never be the thing that breaks a semester.
- Classes are recorded. Recordings are for catching up on a week you missed,
  not for attending.

## What the room decides

Some of this course is not set by the teaching staff, and the Ed forum is
where those decisions get made.

- **Where the lecture happens.** Lectures are outdoors and the venue moves.
  Suggest places and vote; the winner is posted by the Friday before. The only
  hard constraints are that everyone can get there and everyone can arrive on
  time.
- **Which techniques we try.** After
  [the anonymous thread](/assessments/the-anonymous-thread/), every technique
  students reported gets collected, stripped of identifying detail, and put to
  a vote. The ones the room finds most interesting get tried in class, one per
  week, for the rest of semester.
- **What gets a lecture.** If there is a topic you want covered properly,
  propose it on the forum. Proposals get voted on and the ones with real
  support are worked up into later lectures. Several of the current ones
  arrived this way.

**How the voting works.** Every vote runs as an Ed forum poll, which is where
the one-person-one-vote part comes from: the forum knows who you are, so it
will not let you vote twice, and this site could not enforce that on its own.
This site is a set of static pages with no login and no server behind it — a
poll embedded here would either be open to anyone on the internet or
un-auditable, so the vote lives on the forum and the *result* is posted here
once it closes. Live running totals stay on the forum while a poll is open.

None of these votes are decorative. If the room picks something the teaching
staff think is a dead end, we will say so on the page and run it anyway.

## Extensions and late work

Two of the deadlines are already soft, and you do not have to ask for those:

- **[The anonymous thread](/assessments/the-anonymous-thread/)** is due at the
  end of the first week of the mid-semester break, and you can take it through
  to **the end of the break**. The early date exists so the thread has
  something in it to reply to, not to catch anyone out.
- **[The log](/assessments/the-log/)** has **a day or two** past its deadline,
  no questions asked. It is the last thing the course collects; manufacturing
  a rush over it would be absurd.

For anything else: ask. Before the deadline if you can manage it, after if you
couldn't. You do not need a document, a diagnosis, or a paragraph explaining
yourself. The work in this course is deliberately scheduled away from
everyone else's crunch precisely so that extensions are rarely necessary — but
lives do not read the assessment calendar, and asking costs you nothing.

## A standing caveat

This course is taught by people who have not mastered any of this.

The title has two readings and both are meant. A spiral is unfinished thinking
— that is the argument. But so is this course, and so is the research it
draws on: half the studies cited here are small, several are contested, and
the course's own central claim has never been tested directly. Nobody has
finished thinking about any of it.

Quality of life is not a thing anyone finishes either. Nobody in this room —
staff included — has stopped overthinking, and a course that implied otherwise
would be selling something. What is on offer is a set of techniques with honestly
stated evidence, a room in which to practise them, and the expectation that
you will keep learning about this long after the semester ends, from sources
that have nothing to do with us.

Which is also why [the log](/assessments/the-log/) asks what you found
*elsewhere* that worked better than anything we taught you. Those answers
change the syllabus. Staying humble about this is not modesty; it is the only
posture that keeps the course honest.

## Where to get help

**The Ed forum is the best place, and it is not a last resort.** Most questions
have already been asked there, answers stay visible for everyone who has the
same problem next week, and you can post anonymously. If you are deciding
between the forum and an email, post on the forum.

After that, in rough order of how fast you will hear back:

| Who | Good for | Speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ed forum** | Anything. Genuinely anything. | Fastest — other students answer too |
| **Your tutor** — see [People](/people/) | The crits, the assessments, something that came up in class | Same or next day |
| **The convenor** | Extensions, attendance, anything about how the course is run, anything you would rather not post | A day or two |
| **Revathi Menon**, our visiting meditation teacher | Questions about the practice itself | **Slow — do not wait on it.** She is here for one class and teaches elsewhere; a reply may take a while or not come at all |

That last row is not discouragement — she is happy to be asked, and it is the
only way to get an answer from someone who actually works in the tradition.
Just do not let a question sit unanswered in her inbox when the forum would
have solved it in an hour. Best of all, ask her in week 3 while she is
standing in front of you.

For anything about the course itself, asking in the room where it came up
beats an email a week later.

## Support beyond the course

- **ANU Counselling** — free, confidential, and appropriate for anything this
  course opens up that you would rather not handle alone.
- **Access and Inclusion** — for adjustments, including to how attendance
  works for you.

Nothing in this course is therapy or a substitute for it. It is genuinely
useful and it is not treatment, and the distinction matters most exactly when
you would prefer to ignore it.
