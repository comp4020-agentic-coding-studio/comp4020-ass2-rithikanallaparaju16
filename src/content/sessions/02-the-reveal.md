## Before the session

Arrive with a genuinely small thing you spent disproportionate energy
overthinking — a text, an outfit, a reply-all, whether a look meant
something — and, crucially, know how it actually turned out.

Small is the operative word. Anything you would not want the room laughing at
is the wrong item for today, and picking a light one is not dodging the
exercise — the mechanism is the same at every size, and it is much easier to
see when nothing is at stake.

## In the session

One at a time, someone describes the situation and the spiral, stopping right
before the outcome. The room guesses what happened. Then the reveal. Repeat
until everyone who wants a turn has had one — and "who wants a turn" is meant
literally. Passing costs nothing and needs no reason.

After a few rounds, pause on the pattern rather than the punchline: what did
the guesses tend to get wrong, and in which direction? Watch, without
asserting it up front, for the usual shape — guesses skew toward the version
that was overthought, not the version that actually happened.

## Afterwards

Nothing to submit, but keep this one in mind for your log — the same
gap, between what got built up and what actually happened, is the raw
material for that assessment.
