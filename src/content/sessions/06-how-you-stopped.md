## Before the session

[The reveal](/sessions/02-the-reveal/) collected the spirals. This one
collects the exits.

Come with something that actually stopped one — not the thing you think should
work, and not something you read. The real one. Setting a two-minute timer.
Saying it out loud in a stupid voice. Texting the person and finding out. Your
housemate saying "just send it" in a tone that ended the conversation.
Standing up. Going to sleep. Deciding to be wrong on purpose.

They are usually undignified. That is the whole reason this session exists as
a session and not a reading list: the effective ones do not survive being
written down seriously, so they mostly go unshared.

## In the session

Round one, quick: everyone gives theirs in a sentence or two. What happened,
what you did, whether it worked. No analysis yet, and no defending it — "I
don't know why that worked" is a complete answer.

Round two: we sort them on the board and look for the shapes. Some rough
categories to start from, which you should argue with:

- **Time-boxing** — a limit imposed from outside the thinking
- **Making it external** — saying it aloud, writing it, telling a person
- **Interrupting the body** — moving, cold water, leaving the room
- **Ending the uncertainty** — just asking, just sending, just finding out
- **Making it ridiculous** — the stupid voice, the deliberately absurd
  worst-case, naming the spiral out loud

Round three: pair up, and each of you picks one technique from someone else's
list to actually try before the next crit. Not the one you liked best — the
one you would never have thought of.

## The bit worth noticing

Several of these will be versions of the defusion stem from
[week 1](/lectures/01-not-that-serious/) — "I'm having the thought that…" —
arrived at independently and phrased much better. That is a good sign about
both the technique and the room. Where the group has reinvented something with
a name, we will name it afterwards, which is the order this course does things
in.

Also worth noticing: how many of these are ways of *ending uncertainty* rather
than ways of thinking better. Whether that is a cheat or the actual answer is
the argument to have with the time remaining.

## Afterwards

Report back next crit on the technique you borrowed, in one sentence,
including if it did nothing. A technique that failed on you is more useful to
the room than a third person confirming that timers work.
