## The whole technique is six words

Cognitive defusion, from Steven Hayes' work on acceptance and commitment
therapy.

It does not argue with the thought, disprove it, or replace it with a nicer
one — those all take the thought seriously enough to fight it.

It puts a gap between the thought and you. It sounds ridiculous the first few
times. That is fine.

## Aim it at the good

Run the full layered-analysis machinery on the **best realistic** version of
something you are waiting on.

Same rigour. Same refusal to stop early.

Watch what resistance shows up — *this feels indulgent*, *isn't this just
wishful thinking* — and treat it as data rather than a reason to stop.

## Act anyway

Shoma Morita, Japan, c.1920, for *shinkeishitsu* — a nervous,
self-scrutinising disposition.

Western approaches reduce the feeling first, then act. Morita's position:
unpleasant feelings are **natural features of having emotions, not
malfunctions to remove.** Accept the weather; do what needs doing.

*Pilot RCT: Sugg, Richards and Frost, 2018 — promising, not established.*

## Other people close your loops

Chibanda and colleagues (2016, *JAMA*), Zimbabwe: six sessions of
problem-solving therapy delivered by trained grandmothers, on wooden benches
outside clinics, in Shona.

Greater reductions in depression and anxiety than usual care, still there at
six months.

**Built for its context rather than imported into it.** And the mechanism is
another person, not a technique you apply to yourself.

---

## Borrowed and renamed

The ten minutes was not invented by the researchers who measured it.

Kabat-Zinn (2011) does not hide the lineage — and Husgafvel (2019) shows the
tidy "MBSR = Vipassana" story is too simple: Mahāyāna, Zen and Dzogchen
teachers all shaped it.

The accurate sentence is the less quotable one: **adapted from Buddhist
contemplative traditions, plural, and then renamed.**

## The pattern to watch for

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | A practice lives in a community, with a teacher and obligations |
| 2 | A Western populariser compresses it into a graphic or a mantra |
| 3 | It recirculates as ancient authenticity, minus the community |
| 4 | The point moves from *repairing something between people* to *optimising an individual* |

The four-circle *ikigai* was made by a British blogger in 2014. Four-phrase
hoʻoponopono is a 2000s self-help product.

## A framework that disagrees with this course

Gee, Dudgeon, Schultz, Hart and Kelly (2014): Aboriginal and Torres Strait
Islander social and emotional wellbeing, built on **seven domains of
connection** — body; mind and emotions; family and kinship; community;
culture; Country; spirituality and ancestors.

Mind and emotions is *one* of seven. Almost everything this course does lives
in that single domain.

Not resolved here. Deliberately left standing.
