Second of the cohort's picks, and another one that came up in the thread under
several names. "I have a rule about it." Treat the plan below as pencil, not
ink. The forum can still move things around in the weeks that follow.

## Before the session

Bring three triggers. Not three worries, three *triggers*. The moment just
before it starts. Opening the laptop at 11pm. Seeing a name in the inbox.
Leaving a room where you said something. Getting into bed.

Specific ones. "When I feel anxious" is not a trigger, it is the weather.

## In the session

We write plans in one shape, and only one.

> **If** [this exact situation], **then** I will [this exact thing].

The activity comes first and the name comes later, so spend the first half
just writing them. Three each, minimum. Then we stress-test them against each
other in pairs, and your partner's only job is to break yours. There are three
standard ways a plan breaks.

- **The cue is too vague to fire.** You will never notice "when I start
  overthinking", because noticing is the hard part.
- **The action is too big.** "Then I will do forty minutes of journalling" is
  a plan for a better person on a better day.
- **The plan is a wish.** "Then I will stop" is not an action. It is the
  outcome you wanted, with the word "then" in front.

Rewrite until your partner cannot break it. Then swap.

Last round, out loud and optional. Read one plan to the room. You can pass,
and you can read a plan you have deliberately made generic. Nobody is going to
ask what the trigger really was.

## The thing worth noticing

Everyone in this room has decided to overthink less. It has not worked. The
claim behind this crit is that the deciding was never the missing piece, and
that what is missing is a cue specific enough to fire on its own, attached to
an action small enough to survive a bad day.

[The lecture this week](/lectures/09-what-makes-it-work/) has the research and
the honest size of the effect. It runs after this on purpose, because the
course names things after you have already made them.

## Afterwards

Carry the three plans into the week. One line in the log per plan, saying
whether the cue fired at all. A plan whose cue never fired is not a failed
plan, it is a badly chosen trigger, and that distinction is the whole skill.
