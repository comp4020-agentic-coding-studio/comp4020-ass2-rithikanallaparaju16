## Why this is in an overthinking course

Not for general wellbeing, and not because it is good for you in the way
vegetables are. It is here because it is the one practice in this course with
evidence pointing at **rumination specifically** — the exact thing the other
crits have you catching in the act.

Jain and colleagues (2007) ran a randomised trial and found mindfulness
meditation reduced rumination more than relaxation training did, which is the
comparison that matters: it is not simply the effect of sitting down and
being calm for a while. A 2022 meta-analysis of trials in depressed patients
found a reduction in rumination that was real and small-to-moderate.

Small-to-moderate is the honest size. Ten minutes is not going to end your
spirals, nobody here will claim it does, and the week 1 lecture already noted
that the better-controlled trials found no structural brain changes at all.
What it plausibly buys you is a slightly earlier noticing — which, in a course
whose entire premise is catching the loop while it is happening, is the thing
worth having.

## Before the session

Nothing to prepare, and nothing to read. Wear something you can sit on grass
in.

## In the session

Nadia Okonkwo, who teaches this for a living, runs the ten minutes properly —
posture, breath, what to do when you notice you have wandered off, and why
"clearing your mind" is not the instruction and never was. We have been
approximating this for two weeks; the point of today is to stop approximating.

The remaining forty minutes are questions, and they should be the sceptical
ones. A partial list of things worth asking someone who actually knows:

- What is the difference between meditating and sitting there having thoughts?
- Is ten minutes long enough to be anything other than a gesture?
- What does it feel like when it is going badly, and is that a problem?
- What do you do when sitting still makes the spiral *louder*, which is what
  it does for some people?
- What do you make of the evidence above being as modest as it is?

That last one is not a trap. A practitioner's answer to a small-to-moderate
result is more interesting than a press release's.

## Afterwards

Every class from here opens with these ten minutes, and the invitation — not
the requirement — is to do them daily as well. Ten minutes is chosen because
it is small enough that skipping it is hard to justify and short enough that
nobody has to reorganise their morning.

Nobody checks. There is no log, no streak, and no part of your mark that
depends on it. If you decide after four weeks that it does nothing for you,
that is a genuinely useful finding and the audit is a good place to say so.

If sitting still with your own thoughts is not a neutral experience for you —
and for some people it isn't — the pass rule applies here as it does
everywhere. Sit it out, walk, or arrive ten minutes late by arrangement, with
no explanation owed to anyone.
