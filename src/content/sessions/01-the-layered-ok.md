## Before the session

Bring your phone, unlocked, with a messages app open — or a memory of a
message that took you longer to read than to write. Nothing else to prepare:
there is no reading for this one.

## In the session

Someone puts a plain "OK" (no punctuation, no emoji) on the screen, sent in
response to something ordinary. The group reads it aloud, then takes it
apart: what does it mean, what does it *not* mean, what would change if it
had a full stop, if it came a minute later, if it came an hour later. Each
round adds a layer; nobody is allowed to resolve it, only to add another
possible reading.

When the layers run out — or when the room notices it has started arguing
about something other than the message — stop, and go back through the
transcript together. At which layer did the group stop learning anything new
about the message, and start projecting? There is no correct answer here,
only the exercise of noticing the seam.

Then, quickly: everyone names one real "OK" (or its equivalent) they have
personally over-read, and where — with the benefit of hindsight — they would
have called it a day.

## Afterwards

Nothing to submit. What should leave the room is a personal marker: a phrase
or feeling you can use later to recognise "I am now on the layer past useful"
while it is happening, not just in hindsight.
