The [lectures](/lectures/) and [crits](/sessions/) are listed by week, which
is the right order to *do* them in and the wrong order to look something up
in. This page is the same material grouped by subject, so you can find the
thing you half-remember without working out which Tuesday it was on.

## Catching yourself

The symptom, and the first four weeks. No framework, just the thing you
already recognise.

- [The layered "OK"](/sessions/01-the-layered-ok/) — the room overanalyses a
  two-letter reply until it runs out of road, then works out where it could
  have stopped
- [The reveal](/sessions/02-the-reveal/) — you describe a spiral and stop
  before the ending; the room guesses what actually happened
- [Why the spiral feels like work](/lectures/02-the-spiral/) — rumination
  versus reflection, and why worry is so hard to put down
- [How you stopped](/sessions/06-how-you-stopped/) — the undignified things
  that genuinely broke a loop, collected and sorted

## Why a loop repeats

The course's actual claim, and the research it rests on.

- [The empty slot](/lectures/05-the-empty-slot/) — incubation, attention
  residue, and the argument that thinking repeats because it never finishes
- [The blank that isn't](/lectures/06-the-blank-that-isnt/) — doomscrolling,
  and why the thing that feels like relief is the wrong kind of occupied
- [When it helps](/sessions/11-when-it-helps/) — the counter-case, where more
  analysis really was the right call

## Making room

Building the conditions where a thought can finish. This is the half of the
course that looks least like a course.

- [Ten minutes, guided](/sessions/03-ten-minutes-guided/) — the one practice
  with evidence pointing at rumination specifically, run by someone who
  teaches it
- [Deliberately slow](/sessions/04-deliberately-slow/) — three ordinary
  actions at half speed, including the one the evidence says to do faster
- [The sandwich](/sessions/05-the-sandwich/) — an incubation slot built on
  purpose, in silence, with nothing else running
- [The nap, the coffee and the sweet treat](/lectures/10-nap-coffee-treat/) —
  what actually helps under load, and which of the three backfires

## Aiming it, and taking it less seriously

- [Nothing here is that serious](/lectures/01-not-that-serious/) — cognitive
  defusion, the ten-minute detox, and how this room works
- [Aim it at the good](/sessions/07-aim-it-at-the-good/) — the same
  over-analysis pointed at a best case instead of a worst one
- [Act anyway](/lectures/08-act-anyway/) — Morita therapy's refusal to wait
  until you feel better, and the Friendship Bench

## Where the ideas come from

- [Borrowed and renamed](/lectures/04-borrowed-and-renamed/) — the provenance
  of everything here, two invented traditions to avoid, and a framework that
  disagrees with this course
- [Policies and support](/policies/) — the AI rule, attendance, and the
  standing caveat that nobody has mastered any of this

## Still open

Not a topic so much as an admission. Some weeks are deliberately unbooked:
after [the anonymous thread](/assessments/the-anonymous-thread/) the cohort
votes on which student-reported techniques the class tries, and those become
crits in the back half of semester. You can also propose a lecture topic on
the forum and have it voted up.

So this list is expected to grow, and where it grows is not up to the
teaching staff. Which is either fitting for a course called Unfinished
Thinking or an excuse. Both, probably.
