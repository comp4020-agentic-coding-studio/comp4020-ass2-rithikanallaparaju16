One lecture a week, paired with that week's [crit](/sessions/). They carry the
course's argument — that a spiral repeats because the thinking never gets the
conditions under which it would finish — and the real research that argument
rests on. They also mark, repeatedly, the point where the popular version of
that research runs ahead of the evidence. Any claim on these pages with a name
and a year attached is a paper someone here has read. Anything without one is
a hunch, and is labelled as a hunch.

All of them are held **outdoors**, in a spot posted here by the Friday before.
**You pick the spot** — suggest places on the Ed forum and vote; the only hard
constraints are that everyone can get there and everyone can arrive on time.
Because there is nowhere to wait around, these classes start on time and
cannot start late.

You also get a say in what they cover. If there is a topic you want treated
properly, propose it on the forum: proposals are voted on, and the ones with
real support get worked up into later lectures. Several of the ones below
arrived that way.

Each lecture opens with the same ten minutes of guided attention that every
crit opens with — see
[Nothing here is that serious](/lectures/01-not-that-serious/) for what that is
and why it has a slightly silly name.
