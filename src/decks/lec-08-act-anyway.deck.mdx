# So far we have assumed two things

The work happens in your head. And you do it alone.

## Before the theory

You are anxious about starting something. In one line, what do you try to do
first?

Almost every answer is a version of the same move. Challenge the thought.
Reframe it. Relax. Breathe. Get the anxiety down, and *then* begin.

---

## Morita disagreed, in 1920

Shoma Morita (1874–1938) developed his approach in Japan around 1920, for a
condition he called *shinkeishitsu*. Roughly, a nervous and self-scrutinising
disposition.

His position was that unpleasant feelings are **natural features of having
emotions, not malfunctions to be removed**. Trying to eliminate them is futile,
and it is the thing that keeps you fixated on them.

## The classical treatment

| Stage | What it involved |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Absolute bed rest, no reading, no talking, no distraction |

| 3 | Heavier, more demanding work |
| 4 | Return to social life and ordinary activity |

## A parallel, and a warning about it

A century before anyone measured incubation during undemanding tasks, Morita
was prescribing enforced emptiness followed by light monotonous work.

**That parallel is mine and it is not evidence of anything.** Two traditions
arriving at similar practice is interesting, not confirmatory.

---

## The evidence, honestly

Sugg, Richards and Frost ran a pilot randomised trial of Morita therapy in the
UK (2018, *BMJ Open*). 60 participants, Morita plus usual care against usual
care alone.

Enough promise to justify going further. The authors calculated that a full
trial would need around 133 people per arm.

**A promising pilot, not an established treatment.**

## The Friendship Bench

Dixon Chibanda and colleagues (2016, *JAMA*) trained lay health workers, in
practice grandmothers, to deliver six sessions of problem-solving therapy.

Wooden benches outside primary care clinics, in Shona, in local idiom rather
than translated clinical language.

Randomised by clinic. Substantially greater reductions in depression and
anxiety than usual care, still there at six months.

---

## Three things worth more than the effect size

- **Built for its context, not imported into it.** The design started from who
  was available and what language people actually spiral in.
- **The mechanism is another person.** Not a technique you apply to yourself.
- **It is a bench, outdoors.** Zimbabwe put it outside because that is where
  people would sit down and talk.

---

## What this does to our argument

The course has treated overthinking as a private mechanical problem with a
private mechanical fix.

Morita says the fix is not mechanical. The bench says it is not private.

Neither is in the course's central claim, and both have at least as much
support as it does.

## Discussion

- Morita would say this course's noticing exercises risk making you *more*

- "Act anyway" and "you can always pass" are not obviously compatible. Where
  is the line?
- If another person is the active ingredient, what is the honest version of
