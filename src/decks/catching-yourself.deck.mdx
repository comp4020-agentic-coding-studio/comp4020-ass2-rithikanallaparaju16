# Catching yourself

Weeks 1–4. The part you already recognise.

# You are not thinking too much

You are thinking in a loop that never reaches an end.

---

## The layered "OK"

One two-letter reply. The room adds readings until it runs out of road.

- nobody is allowed to resolve it
- only to add another possible meaning
- then: at which layer did we stop learning about the message and start
  projecting?

There is no correct answer. Noticing the seam is the exercise.

---

## Rumination is not reflection

Nolen-Hoeksema's distinction is about *mode*, not amount.

| Mode of thinking | What it is called |
| --- | --- |
| Circling the problem, rehearsing the framing | rumination |
| Moving toward something | reflection |

So the useful question is never "am I thinking too much." It is **is this loop
adding anything, and would I notice if it stopped.**

---

## Why it is hard to put down

Borkovec's cognitive-avoidance model: worry is verbal, and being verbal is the
point — it sidesteps the imagery and the physical response a feeling needs in
order to be processed.

It feels like work because it *is* work. Just not the work that resolves
anything.

**One leading theory, with live rivals.** Not a settled cause.

---

## The reveal

Describe the spiral. Stop before the ending. Let the room guess.

Then look at the pattern rather than the punchline: the guesses skew toward
the version that was overthought, not the version that happened.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> I didn't get out of it. It just eventually stopped mattering.

A complete and honest answer, and more useful to the room than a tidy one.

---

## How you stopped

The exits people actually use, sorted:

- **time-boxing** — a limit from outside the thinking
- **making it external** — saying it aloud, writing it, telling someone
- **interrupting the body** — moving, cold water, leaving
- **ending the uncertainty** — just asking, just sending
- **making it ridiculous** — the stupid voice, naming the spiral

Notice how many are ways of *ending* something rather than thinking better.
