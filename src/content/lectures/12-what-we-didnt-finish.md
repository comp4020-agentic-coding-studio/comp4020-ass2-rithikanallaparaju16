## The accounting

A course called Unfinished Thinking that ended with a summary would be
getting its own joke wrong. So here is the ledger instead.

**The central claim is untested.** That a loop repeats because the thinking
never gets the conditions under which it would finish. It is assembled from
real findings, incubation during undemanding tasks, attention residue, the
phone break that measures like no break. Nobody has tested the claim
directly, including us. It is the best account we could build, and it is
still a hunch with good parts.

**Three things we refused to claim.** They are listed on
[the deck](/decks/why-a-loop-repeats/) and they have not moved all semester.
The temptation to quietly upgrade them at the end of a course is exactly what
this lecture exists to resist.

**Two traditions poked holes we never filled.**
[Week 8](/lectures/08-act-anyway/) gave you Morita, saying the fix is not
mechanical, and the Friendship Bench, saying it is not private. Both have at
least as much support as the course's own claim. Neither is in it.

**Most of it is small.** [Week 9](/lectures/09-what-makes-it-work/) put the
numbers in one table. Worry postponement, d = 0.31 and 0.19. Expressive
writing, about d = 0.15. Exercise on state anxiety, a quarter of a standard
deviation. The honest summary of a semester is that several of these help a
bit, one of them measurably backfires, and the biggest number on the
syllabus is also the oldest and least re-examined.

**Some of it we got wrong on the way and fixed in public.** The sequencing of
this course was built on concrete-to-abstract evidence, then corrected
against Luiten, Ames and Ackerson's meta-analysis, which says learners do
better with a brief organising idea up front. That is why week 1 states the
argument in a sentence and then abandons it until week 5. The
[Lectures](/lectures/) page carries the full correction, including the
contested paper that says the whole approach might be backwards.

**One thing we could not find at all.** The assessment calendar in this
course is built around avoiding everyone else's deadlines. We could not find
a study isolating clustered against spread deadlines and measuring wellbeing.
[Assessment](/assessments/) labels it design reasoning, not a finding,
because that is what it is.

## What actually transfers

Strip the content out and three things survive.

**Noticing the seam.** [Week 1](/sessions/01-the-layered-ok/) asked at which
layer the group stopped learning about the message and started projecting.
That question works on anything, and it does not need any of the research to
be true.

**Checking what you are crediting.** You had a break and credited the sugar.
That error is everywhere once you can see it, and it is most of what
separates a technique that works from one you believe works.

**Knowing what would change your mind.** The last crit made you state a
prediction specific enough to be wrong. The same move applies to every claim
in this course, including ours.

## Where to keep going

Not here. [The log](/assessments/the-log/) asks what you found *elsewhere*
that worked better than anything we taught you, and those answers change the
syllabus. That is not a nice gesture at the end of semester. Three of the
crits you sat this year came out of a previous cohort answering that
question.

Nobody teaching this has stopped overthinking. That was on the
[Policies](/policies/) page from week 1 and it is still true in week 12.
