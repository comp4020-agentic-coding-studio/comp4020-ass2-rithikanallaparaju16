Four lectures across the semester, not twelve. They carry the course's
argument — that a spiral repeats because the thinking never gets the
conditions under which it would finish — and the real research that argument
rests on. They also mark, repeatedly, the point where the popular version of
that research runs ahead of the evidence. Any claim on these pages with a name
and a year attached is a paper someone here has read. Anything without one is
a hunch, and is labelled as a hunch.

All three are held **outdoors**, in a spot posted here by the Friday before.
You can suggest venues. Because there is nowhere to wait around, they start on
time and cannot start late.

Each one opens with the same ten minutes of guided attention that every crit
opens with — see [Nothing here is that serious](/lectures/01-not-that-serious/)
for what that is and why it has a slightly silly name.
